# Reference-inspired alien battlefield

The three supplied environment references inform a warm industrial battlefield, a huge weathered spacecraft drive and a colder scorched basin. The implementation adds a playable crash field near the existing canyon rather than claiming the entire enormous map has received a bespoke art pass.

- **AEGIS Strike:** newly authored Blender armor, red optics, articulated mechanical frame and scanned surface detail.
- **ORPHEUS–7:** a hollow spacecraft drive with broken panels, internal ribs and a continuous entrance ramp/deck. The title screen's “探索坠舰荒原 · 新” deploys the robot here. World position 5500/6500; hull scale 22 game units per source unit. `exo-wreck-world.js` supplies matching ground, shell, projectile and flight support.
- **Terrain:** an eroded crater rim is part of the physical terrain, with a north approach and no change to the authored canyon's interior surface. Poly Haven's [Burned Ground 01](https://polyhaven.com/a/burned_ground_01), by Rob Tuytel, CC0-1.0, supplies 2K color/normal/roughness maps. Ash blends with existing rock/soil scans at multiple scales. Smaller debris is smoothed and instanced.
- **Atmosphere:** a low warm sun, cooler ambient light, layered moving cloud banks, a visible ringed planet, distance haze, localized smoke, flames and embers. The basin around 13400/8000 uses a cooler storm palette. Existing underground spaces keep their interior lighting and hide surface sky/effects.

Powered by Poly Haven. No Megascans purchase or paid generation service was used. Asset URLs, authors and licenses are retained alongside the downloaded files. Browser rendering is still subject to device performance; the game menu exposes measured frame rate and draw statistics.

## Validation

The actual exported robot has 47,192 triangles and the optimized wreck has 93,979. Automated checks cover the exported geometry, material assignment, robot poses, weapons, flight, world collisions, ramp/deck route, camera obstruction and existing canyon/facility behavior. Browser QA at the native 1280 × 720 viewport confirmed entry along the ramp, walking inside, camera orbit, firing controls, roof takeoff restriction and outdoor flight. No browser warnings or errors were reported. The warmed outdoor preview reported 21 FPS, 276 draws and 421,336 triangles; further performance work is needed before treating this as a high-frame-rate release.

## Combat and creature implementation

The approved GPT Image sheets in `creature-concepts-v1/` and `combat-vfx-concepts-v1/` guide playable procedural Three.js models and effects. They are design references, not runtime 3D assets or a claim of matching the illustrations' rendering quality.

- Four silhouettes: six-legged Hunter, horned Charger, Spore Artillery with translucent sacs and a dorsal mortar, and Brood Lord with a crown and paired scythes. Faceted mineral armor covers articulated tendons. Terrain-sampled feet, distance-driven walking, and separate windup/release/recovery poses improve motion readability.
- The six-legged Void Leviathan has a segmented body, long tail, mandibles, charge rings, and a throat-to-player beam. The existing immediate boundary defeat and safe-camp recovery remain authoritative.
- The robot has a staged magenta saber cut with a blade-following trail, cyan pulse fire with recoil and muzzle compression rings, and delayed missile salvos with opening pod doors, visible missiles, exhaust and smoke. Impacts include sparks, debris, rings and fading smoke.
- Enemy projectiles originate at animated attack sockets. Mortar arcs, corrosive pools, charge lanes, impact bursts and shockwaves follow the actual combat state. Reduced-motion settings simplify cosmetic motion and particles.
- Shared instanced effects have fixed capacities; creatures use rigid skinning and distance LOD. Near models contain approximately 38–43k triangles and use 2–5 draws each; far models contain approximately 9–10k. The guardian uses approximately 56k triangles and six draws including effects.
- The title's **战斗试炼 · 新** and the in-game **异兽与武器试炼** menu provide isolated encounters with each species and a safe boundary observation point. Leaving the trial restores the surface population.

Final checks passed for creature geometry and poses, weapon socket effects, effect capacity/lifecycle, attack phases, controls/trial restoration, flight, aerial combat, world details and boundary behavior. Browser QA at 1280 × 720 verified monster attacks, cannon/missile use, Leviathan charging, immediate boundary defeat and its visible beam. No browser warnings or errors were reported. Versioned module URLs were advanced after QA exposed a cached old guardian. The Brood Lord scene measured about 16 FPS, 159 draws and 630,017 triangles on this preview; this remains a prototype requiring further performance and material work.

## Performance pass: unchanged visual settings

The renderer now resizes its drawing buffer only when the viewport changes, and rebuilds static world markers only when their actual content changes. Identical terrain and creature vertices share indices; positions, normals, colors, skin weights, UVs and triangle counts are retained. The robot's 67 original armor pieces use eight material batches. Large opaque canyon and wreck meshes are divided into independently culled ranges inside batched draws. Rock instances are culled against both the camera and the sun's shadow frustum so offscreen shadow casters remain available. The full-resolution procedural sky renders after opaque geometry, allowing depth rejection of hidden pixels.

A background worker prepares the same full-detail terrain buffers ahead of travel. The current ring always has a synchronous fallback, so worker failure or late delivery cannot leave missing ground. Menus and hidden tabs suspend rendering, with frame timing reset on resume. Texture sizes, antialiasing, pixel ratio, shadow-map resolution, model detail thresholds, cloud shader detail and combat effects were not reduced.

Validation includes exact expanded-vertex comparisons, original robot transforms/materials across walk/aim/recoil/saber/flight poses, conservative shadow culling, streamed-buffer parity for the surface/wreck/canyon/basin and both caves, and ray-hit parity for the authored canyon (202,062 triangles) and wreck (93,979). Fifteen relevant automated suites passed. Browser checks covered combat effects, boost flight across terrain cells, canyon visuals, and rendering suspension; no browser warnings or errors were reported. A flight sample used 126 prepared tiles with zero synchronous misses.

Measurements used the native 1280 × 720 preview on Intel Iris Plus via ANGLE/Metal. Early baseline windows showed about 20–21 FPS, 159–164 draws, approximately 630k triangles and about 10–12 ms of CPU work. The first optimization stage reached roughly 26–27 FPS. Later, other heavy applications were running; the user confirmed this and requested they remain running. Under that concurrent load, the original path measured about 4.9 FPS and the optimized Brood Lord scene measured 9.4–9.9 FPS, around 88–91 draws and 430k submitted triangles. Optimized CPU samples were approximately 4.5–7 ms. Background load was not controlled, so these later FPS windows are not a clean speedup benchmark or a claim of smooth 30/60 FPS. Captured recent diagnostics are retained in `performance-v1/measurements.json` and `performance-v1/final-measurements.json`. Local-only `?profile=1` enables stage/GPU timings for future investigation.

## Full-quality GPU pass, 2026-10-31 (v48)

The second performance pass retains the 1.25 render-pixel-ratio cap, hardware antialiasing, 1024-pixel soft shadows, authored triangles, original materials, 2K scans, animated sky, and combat/flight effects.

- A native-resolution opaque depth prepass rejects covered fragments before expensive PBR/scan shading. Transparent surfaces and effects retain their original color pass; materials with custom fragment discards explicitly opt out. Camera, material, layer, clear, and shadow state is restored, including errors.
- Five derived PNG textures losslessly pack original normal RGB + roughness G into RGBA, or original scan RGB + height R into RGBA. Normal/roughness and triplanar albedo/height share samples. `scripts/pack-material-channels.py` checks every decoded source byte. Texture dimensions, UV transforms, anisotropy, mipmaps, and color-space handling remain unchanged. This reduces resident GPU scan textures, at the cost of larger lossless asset downloads than the original JPEGs; original source JPEGs remain in the repository.
- Static canyon/wreck batches use their known identity transform directly in color/depth/shadow shaders, avoiding redundant per-vertex matrix-texture fetches. Authored vertex positions and collision surfaces are unchanged.
- Rock instance visibility is separate for the camera and the light. Offscreen shadow casters remain in the shadow draw without entering the camera depth/color draws. Unchanged instance selections no longer upload their buffers each frame.
- Local-only `?profile=1&benchmark=1` holds a deterministic scene/time for comparison. Normal public play cannot enable these diagnostics. Production always uses the optimized renderer; experimental masking and alternate draw-order switches were removed.

Validation: 18 Node suites passed, including expanded-vertex/triangle and collision parity, animation/combat/flight, both cave tile buffers, independent camera/shadow visibility, render-state restoration on errors, packed material equations, and the actual authored canyon. Native browser checks covered live cannon/missiles/saber, boosted flight, canyon, and HELIOS entry/walking; no runtime/shader warnings or errors were observed. A fixed 1280x720 combat screenshot comparison against the old build differed by about 0.009/255 per RGB channel on average, with only 21 of 921,600 pixels differing by more than 10 levels. The packed-texture-only comparison had no pixels differing by more than 10 levels.

Performance is **not yet smooth under the user's competing app load**. An initial fixed-scene baseline measured 10.6 FPS / 58.74 ms GPU; the optimized same scene measured about 13.7–13.9 FPS / 43.1–43.9 ms GPU, roughly 27% lower measured GPU time. Live combat reached 14.2 FPS / 40.75 ms GPU; flight windows ranged roughly 9.5–12.3 FPS and a warmed HELIOS interior measured 11.1 FPS / 34.24 ms GPU. A later foreground run fell to 8.8 FPS even with 45.06 ms GPU and only 5.88 ms CPU work. Initial shader/material warmup also produces stalls. Background apps and system scheduling were uncontrolled throughout; these are observations on this Mac, not a clean FPS speedup guarantee or a 30/60 FPS claim. See `art/performance-v2/browser-measurements.json` for retained samples.
